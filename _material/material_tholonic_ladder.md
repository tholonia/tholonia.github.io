A formal paper demonstrating that five fundamental mathematical constants — π/4, φ (the golden ratio), e, √2, and ln 2 — emerge naturally from a single recursive triadic structure called the tholonic triad. The framework assigns three functionally distinct roles (Negotiation, Definition/Limitation, Contribution/Integration) to every recursion, and shows that the same triadic geometry, traversed with different initial conditions and dynamics, converges to a different fundamental limit each time. The π/4 branch is shown to be equivalent to a Leibniz-type grouped odd-denominator series and is uniquely distinguished as the only branch requiring three independent prime seeds and externally advancing parameters. All other constants are generated from seeds drawn solely from {0, 1, 2}. The five constants presented are recognizable loci in what is, in principle, an infinite landscape of possible traversal paths through the recursive geometry.
